# Cosine Consistency

Spectral reproducibility across library hits is quantified through a metric termed *cosine consistency*, which captures the internal agreement of fragment-ion similarity scores within the dominant structural evidence group for a given query spectrum. For each scan, library hits are first clustered into support groups based on a two-level structural key derived from planar InChIKey and positional-isomer scaffold equivalence; the group that maximises the weighted centroid rank score (WCRS) is designated the winning group. Within this group, the coefficient of variation (CV) of the MQScore (cosine similarity) values is computed as the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean cosine. Cosine consistency is then defined as

$$C_{\text{cos}} = \max\!\left(0,\; 1 - \min(1, \mathrm{CV})\right) = \max\!\left(0,\; 1 - \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\sigma_{\cos}}{\mu_{\cos}}\right)\right)$$

where $\sigma_{\cos}$ and $\mu_{\cos}$ are the standard deviation and mean of the cosine scores across the $n$ hits in the winning group, respectively. Groups containing a single hit are assigned $C_{\text{cos}} = 1.0$ by convention, as no variance can be estimated. The metric is bounded to $[0, 1]$: a value near 1 indicates that all supporting library hits produced highly consistent spectral similarity scores, reflecting robust and reproducible spectral evidence for the proposed annotation; a value near 0 indicates high dispersion among cosine scores within the group, suggesting heterogeneous or ambiguous spectral matching. Cosine consistency is incorporated into the composite confidence score with a nominal weight of 25 out of 110 arbitrary units (≈ 22.7% of the normalised total weight), alongside structure agreement (≈ 45.5%), ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity (≈ 13.6%), best cosine score (≈ 9.1%), and shared fragment peaks (≈ 9.1%).
